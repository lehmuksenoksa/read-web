Read: privacy policy

Updated 14 October 2026

Read is an EPUB reader made by one person for their own use. It has no ads, no analytics and no tracking, and it sells or shares nobody's data.

On your phone

Your books, settings, reading positions and listening history are kept on the phone. Uninstalling Read removes them.

Your Dropbox

If you connect Dropbox, Read reads your books from it and keeps its own files in the app's folder there: reading positions and marks, a list of your books, backups of your settings, and fonts. These files are in your own Dropbox account, under Dropbox's privacy policy; the developer has no access to them. You can delete them in Dropbox at any time.

Problem reports

If Read crashes, it sends a report the next time it starts. You can also send a report yourself. A report contains the app's version, the phone's model and Android version, recent lines from the app's own log, and, for a report you send, your note and a screenshot of the app. Reports go to a private repository on GitHub that only the developer can see, and are used only to fix problems.

Things you ask for

  • Book details: when you look a book up, its title, author or ISBN is sent to Hardcover (hardcover.app).
  • Fonts: when you download a font, it comes from Google Fonts.
  • Stories: when you follow stories on Royal Road or Patreon, Read fetches their pages from those sites. You sign in to Patreon on Patreon's own page, and the sign-in stays on your phone.
  • Reading aloud: uses the speech engine installed on your phone, under its own settings.

Deleting your data

Uninstall Read to remove what's on the phone, and delete the app's folder in your Dropbox to remove what's there. To have reports deleted, write to the address below.
